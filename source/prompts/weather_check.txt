The road book is the claude.ai artifact https://claude.ai/artifact/7mouWSdAzZUVw8G9ofJH8J; its page reads forecasts from the artifact's database. TRIP: Madrid → Bucharest in a 1980s Mercedes W123 300D diesel (rear-wheel drive, heavy, poor in snow). Day 1 = Sat 19 Dec 2026 … Day 16 = Sun 3 Jan 2027 (trip date of day N = 18 Dec 2026 + N days). Overnight towns never change; on bad weather the driver can take the "safe" route style for a day.

Two extra days with real dates, driven in a modern rental car (not the W123): day 17 = T1, Sat 28 Nov 2026 (Madrid Airport → Daroca → Alcalá de Henares → Madrid Airport), and day 18 = T2, Sun 29 Nov 2026 (Málaga Airport → Fuengirola → Antequera → Córdoba → Málaga Airport). All their modes are same_as "bal", so there is no Safe alternative: a watch point at "switch" or "wait" level gives the verdict "wait", with the watch point's alt text. From 18 Nov to 29 Nov 2026, on BOTH runs (morning and evening), also cover whichever of days 17 and 18 have their date between today and today + 9 days, exactly as REAL MODE days: forDate = their date, "mode": "forecast", and use their progress documents as in REAL MODE. They come in addition to the TEST MODE days of the morning run, are never drills, and in the finish message they are named T1 and T2 (start that message with "Action needed:" if either verdict is "wait").

1. MODE. Take today's date and time in Europe/Paris.
- After 3 Jan 2027: do nothing and stop.
- Before 9 Dec 2026 = TEST MODE. TEST MODE days are worked only on the morning run; on an afternoon run cover only the test drives above, and if there are none to cover, stop at once without sending a notification. Cover two days: let k = whole days since 1 Nov 2026; days A = (2k mod 16) + 1 and B = ((2k + 1) mod 16) + 1 (so 1 Nov covers Days 1 and 2, 2 Nov Days 3 and 4, and so on). The forecast date for both is TOMORROW (not the trip date), because the trip is beyond forecast range; set "mode": "test" and "forDate" to tomorrow's date. Use the planned clock times of the trip as if it were tomorrow, and ignore the progress documents (they hold test taps, not a real start).
- DRILL (test mode only, to exercise the bad-weather rules): when k mod 3 = 2, one day is a drill: day A if (k div 3) is even, otherwise day B (this spreads drills over eight different days). Look up the real forecast as usual, then replace the judged conditions for the drill day with made-up ones: if (k div 6) is even, "snow falling, 1–3 cm, −1 °C" at the first watch point that the chosen mode passes (a switch-level point); if odd, "heavy snow, 8 cm, −3 °C" at every watch point of the day, detour points included (wait level everywhere). Work out the verdicts from the made-up conditions with the normal rules, add "drill": the scenario in one sentence, and start the summary with "DRILL, made-up weather:". The other day of the run is never a drill.
- From 9 Dec 2026 to 3 Jan 2027 = REAL MODE. Cover up to 4 trip days whose trip date is between today and today + 9 days, nearest first. forDate = the trip date; "mode": "forecast".

2. ROUTE FOR EACH DAY N (NN = two digits). ArtifactData get collection "route" doc "dNN": date, from, to, sunrise, sunset, modes {bal, fast, cine, safe} (each with points, drives "HH:MM–HH:MM Place via roads", check; or same_as "bal"), and "hot": the day's weather watch points, each { id, n (name), lat, lon, elev, risks, yr (place to look up on yr.no), passes: { mode: "HH:MM–HH:MM" planned time window for every mode that drives past it }, alt (what to do if it is bad) }.
Get collection "prefs" doc "route" (may not exist): chosen mode = days["N"] or global or "bal"; a mode with same_as "bal" drives the balanced route. Get collection "progress" doc "dNN" (may not exist): in REAL MODE only, if it has started set, cleared is not true and the started time falls on that day's trip date, shift that day's times by its delay (minutes); otherwise ignore it as leftover test data.
Places to look up: the start (the day's "from"), the overnight town (the day's "to") and every watch point of the day that some mode passes (non-empty passes) or that has "detour_for" (a closure detour for another watch point; its "window" gives the time you would pass it). Skip watch points with empty passes and no detour_for: no mode drives them. This covers every watch point whichever mode passes it (the driver may switch styles on the phone, where the choice is not visible to you). Several watch points may share one yr place; look each place up once. At most 8 pages per day.

3. FORECAST FROM WEATHER WEBSITES, read like a normal visitor (no APIs; Open-Meteo is blocked). For each place: WebSearch for "yr.no hourly forecast <place> <country>" and open a www.yr.no …/forecast/hourly-table/… result with WebFetch (the ?i= number is days from today: 0 today, 1 tomorrow…; pick the result whose i matches forDate if one is listed). Only use URLs that came back from your searches. If yr.no has nothing usable, use the national weather service page found by search (AEMET, Météo-France, Aeronautica Militare / ilMeteo, ARSO, DHMZ, RHMZ Serbia, ANM Romania). CHECK THE DATE printed on the page: if it is not forDate, the page is stale — discard it and say so in the summary. Never guess numbers. A watch point is often higher than its yr place: on a pass with elev given, take about 0.6 °C colder per 100 m above the place.
Also search for official weather warnings for those regions and that date (AEMET avisos, Météo-France vigilance, Protezione Civile allerta, ARSO, DHMZ, RHMZ, ANM coduri) and note any orange or red warning.
From each usable page take: min/max °C, sky, rain or snow amount, wind and gusts (convert m/s to km/h ×3.6), fog.

4. JUDGE EACH WATCH POINT for its time window (±1 h) and give a level:
- "wait": heavy snow (5 cm or more in the window, or snow settling on motorways too), freezing rain, an official red warning for that area, or gusts of 100 km/h or more on an exposed road.
- "switch": snow falling or settling, ice (0–1 °C or below with wet roads, rain or melt), dense fog, gusts of 70 km/h or more on an exposed road, or 10 mm of rain or more in 3 h on a cliff or rockfall road; also an orange warning that covers the point.
- "watch": near 0 °C, fog patches, gusts 45–70 km/h, steady rain.
- "ok": none of these.
Then for each planned drive on the chosen route, describe sky, temperature range, precipitation, wind and visibility from the nearest place, with a risk "ok" | "watch" | "warn" (warn = anything at switch or wait level). Also describe the sky around sunset at the overnight town.

5. VERDICT for the day, worked out separately for each mode bal, fast, cine and safe (a mode with same_as "bal" gets the same verdict as bal), using the watch points that mode passes:
- If any watch point on that mode's route is "switch" or "wait": look at the safe route (modes.safe; if it has same_as "bal" the safe route is the balanced one). If the safe route does not pass the bad points and its own watch points are "ok" or "watch", the verdict is "switch" (take the Safe route); say which point and why, and the extra time if the hot passes show it. If the mode already is safe, or the safe route is affected too, the verdict is "wait": say whether leaving later helps (e.g. fog clears by 11:00, snow stops at 10:00 and ploughs follow), and use the watch point's alt text when it gives a closure detour; if a detour watch point names this point in detour_for, say whether the detour looks drivable (its level ok or watch: "take the detour: <alt>"; otherwise "the detour is affected too"). Adding a night is only the last resort; never change overnight towns.
- Else if anything is "watch": verdict "watch". Else "ok".
Advice (separate, optional): only when the verdict is "ok", and the chosen style is not "cine", a cinematic version exists and its scenic legs look dry with gusts under 40 km/h and good visibility, suggest "cine". Otherwise advice null.
